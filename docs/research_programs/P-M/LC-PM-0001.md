## identified_mechanisms

**Sub-class:** M1 (inventory) with an explicit order-splitting micro-foundation. Maps to **I5**
(inventory-imbalance liquidity premium) in [[MARKET_INEFFICIENCY_TAXONOMY]].

**Causal chain (constraint → observable):**
large "discretionary liquidity" traders face price impact → **split orders across periods** to
minimise it → order imbalance becomes **serially autocorrelated** → risk-averse market makers with
inventory constraints (risk-aversion coefficient R) accommodate the autocorrelated demand → price
pressure is itself autocorrelated → **lagged imbalance predicts returns**.

**Participant class:** market makers (NYSE specialists), constrained by inventory risk and risk
aversion. Counterparty: order-splitting institutional traders.

**Stated falsification (paper's own logic):** *"The predictability of price movements from imbalances
will not obtain if there are no inventory effects (i.e., if market makers are risk-neutral)."*

## empirical_claims *(recorded for horizon calibration only — §4.1 forbids extracting the finding as evidence)*

| Claim | Value |
|---|---|
| **Horizon of continuation** | lagged imbalance affects returns **up to five days** |
| Sign at lag 1 | positive: 77% of coefficients positive, >25% positive-and-significant |
| **Sign at lags 6–10** | **negative and significant (four of five)** — eventual reversal |
| Contemporaneous relation | strongly positive (OIBNUM 2.326, t=19.35) |
| **Key conditional** | after controlling for *contemporaneous* imbalance, the lagged relation **disappears** |

**market/context:** NYSE only, 1988–1998, ~1,322 stocks/yr. **Quote-driven specialist system.**
**horizon:** 1–5 trading days (continuation); 6–10 days (reversal).

## transportability_condition

The mechanism requires **a constrained inventory-bearing intermediary**. On NYSE that is the
designated specialist. **IDX has no designated market maker** (LC-PM-0006) — the role must be
carried by voluntary liquidity providers, whose risk-bearing capacity is neither observable nor
obligated. Transport therefore requires that IDX's voluntary suppliers behave as constrained
inventory-bearers. Additionally the paper's return measure uses **bid-ask midpoints** to suppress
bounce; a close-to-close IDX measure does not inherit that protection.

## weakest_link

**The conditional in the last row.** Once contemporaneous imbalance is controlled, lagged
predictability vanishes — i.e. the apparent multi-day continuation is largely the *same day's*
displacement measured with a lag, not an independent forward-predictive signal. Any design that
conditions on day-*t* flow and measures day *t→t+k* return is measuring the residual after that
control, which this paper says is near-zero.

## biases[]

- **B7 · Cost omission** — partially mitigated: the paper *does* discuss costs and concludes
  profits are "unlikely to be much larger than brokerage commissions for individual investors."
- **B8 · Market-structure obsolescence** — **severe.** Sample predates decimalization (2001),
  Reg NMS, and the decline of the specialist. The substrate has materially changed.
- **B6 · Period selection** — single 11-year US sample.

## barrier_statement

**Weak.** The barrier is market-maker risk aversion — a **compensation**, not a durable
inefficiency. The paper says so explicitly: the result is *"not necessarily inconsistent with market
efficiency, because the inventory paradigm suggests that buyers may face favorable terms of trade
following days of heavy selling."* Under R17/§6.3 this is fair compensation for risk borne, and
**F1 risk is high** — the primary reason [[LITERATURE_RESEARCH_STANDARD]] §1.3 ranks microstructure
priority 4, not 1.

## limitations

NYSE-only; specialist substrate now obsolete; profits acknowledged marginal net of retail costs;
predictability conditional on not controlling for contemporaneous flow.
