## identified_mechanisms

**Sub-class:** M1 inventory under **time-varying** risk-bearing capacity. **I5 × I6**, with a
state-dependent intensity.

**Causal chain:** market stress → intermediary funding and risk-management constraints tighten
(Gromb–Vayanos, Adrian–Shin) → liquidity supply contracts → required compensation for holding
inventory rises → **negative serial correlation in prices strengthens** → short-horizon reversal
returns rise.

**Participant class:** market makers *de facto* — algorithmic traders and quantitative investors
performing the function without an obligation. **Constraint:** funding, leverage, margin, and
inventory-absorption limits. **This is the modern, non-designated version of the mechanism** and is
therefore the closest specification to what a market without a specialist could support.

**The card's decisive contribution:** the mechanism is **conditional, not unconditional.** Reversal
is not a standing property of the tape; it is a state-dependent price of immediacy.

## empirical_claims *(horizon calibration only)*

| Claim | Value |
|---|---|
| Conditioning variable | **VIX** (normalised, lagged 5 days) — subsumes GARCH vol, vol risk premium, idiosyncratic vol, TED spread, repo growth |
| Sensitivity | +1 pp normalised VIX → **+0.22 pp daily** reversal return |
| Monthly R² | up to **56%** |
| Crisis amplification | reversal returns rose **~10×** from 2006 to Q4 2008 |
| Concentration in stress | small, illiquid, high-volatility stocks |
| **Bid-ask bounce share** | **~50% of profits come from bid-ask bounce**; the remainder from quote-midpoint reversal |

**market/context:** US equities (NYSE/AMEX/Nasdaq, CRSP), Jan 1998 – Dec 2010.
**horizon:** overlaid **five-day** lookback (t−1…t−5); day t−1 alone also works.

## transportability_condition

Requires (a) a **conditioning state variable** — IDX has no VIX analogue; a domestic realised-vol or
IHSG-vol proxy would have to be constructed and is itself untested; and (b) acceptance that the
effect is **absent or weak in calm regimes**, which is most of the sample. An unconditional IDX
reversal test would average across states in which the paper predicts no effect, biasing toward a
null — and a null so produced would be uninformative about the mechanism.

## weakest_link

**The bid-ask bounce finding is the single most dangerous fact in this S1 sweep.** If ~50% of
measured short-horizon reversal profit is mechanical bounce on the *US* tape, then on IDX — with
wider relative spreads on exactly the small illiquid names where the effect concentrates — a naive
close-to-close signed-flow reversal measurement would likely find a **large, significant, entirely
non-harvestable effect.** [[HYP-PM-0001]]'s script already carried a bounce guard (re-measure with a
1-bar entry gap); **any 1–3 day design must inherit an equivalent guard or its result is void.**

Nagel also states plainly that intermediary inventory is unobservable and reversal is used as a
**noisy proxy** for it — the same identification wall as LC-PM-0002.

## biases[]

- **B7 · Cost omission** — **stated by the author**: costs are not evaluated; *"after accounting for
  these fixed costs, Sharpe ratios would likely be much less extreme."*
- **B6 · Period selection** — sample deliberately starts 1998 to avoid Nasdaq order-handling and
  decimalization regime shifts; author notes results require controlling for the 2001 break.
- **B8 · Market-structure obsolescence** — moderate.

## barrier_statement

**The best barrier in the M2.x set, and it is a D3 capital barrier with a name.** Persistence is
explained by *fixed costs of high-speed market access and technology* preventing entry into liquidity
provision, plus binding funding constraints precisely when the compensation is highest. Under §6.3
this is a genuine limits-to-arbitrage barrier: the capital that would compete the premium away is
constrained exactly when the premium appears. **But it is a barrier to entry into market-making —
which is a business IDX retail participants are not in.** Earning it requires supplying immediacy,
not predicting it.

## limitations

Costs unevaluated; intermediary positions unobservable; underlying state variables unidentified and
collinear with VIX; equities only; assumes full cross-sectional diversification of inventory risk
which real providers do not have.
