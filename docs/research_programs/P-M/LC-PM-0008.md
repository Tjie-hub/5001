> **Grade note:** Q3 not Q4 — the empirical design is strong and the rivals are handled, but the
> *mechanism* for why the two sides differ is stated behaviourally and thinly (*"widening the limit
> range provides investors with a greater opportunity to buy (sell) more"*) rather than as a
> complete causal chain with a named constrained class. Usable as evidence and as a design template;
> **not** sufficient on its own to source a registration (§3.1).

## identified_mechanisms

**Sub-class:** M6 origin (limit width) with a behavioural exploitation channel. **I1**, with
**I10 / manipulation** as the named rival.

**Claim:** upper and lower limits are **not mirror images**, and the asymmetry is **not caused by
asymmetric band widths** — the studied change was **symmetric** (±10% → ±20% on both sides), yet
the two sides responded in opposite directions.

- **Upper limit widened:** *alleviates* delayed price discovery and trading interference;
  **increases** volatility spillover, significant through **day 4** post-hit.
- **Lower limit widened:** *exacerbates* delayed price discovery and trading interference; does
  **not** increase volatility spillover; **reduces crashes** — acts as a stabiliser.

**Stated conditioning:** asymmetry is more pronounced under **high sentiment, manipulation risk, and
short-selling eligibility.**

## empirical_claims *(design and horizon calibration only)*

**market/context:** China ChiNext (growth board).
**rule design:** daily limits widened **±10% → ±20%, effective 2020-08-24**.
**identification:** **difference-in-differences** — ChiNext (treated) vs Shanghai/Shenzhen main-board
stocks that retained ±10% (control), with **propensity-score matching** for robustness.
**sample:** approximately four years around the event, extending to 2022-08-23.
**horizon:** volatility spillover significant through day 4.

## Why this card is decisive for the IDX design

It establishes, with a clean DiD, that **response asymmetry between the up-side and the down-side
exists even when the bands themselves are symmetric.** That creates a **rival mechanism** the IDX
study must defeat:

| | Predicts asymmetric next-session response? |
|---|---|
| **H_band** — IDX's −15% vs +20/25/35% band-width asymmetry (M6) | Yes |
| **H_side** — intrinsic up/down asymmetry from shorting constraints, sentiment, manipulation (LC-PM-0007, this card) | **Yes, independently** |

**Band-width asymmetry and side asymmetry are confounded in any single-regime IDX sample.** A study
run only on the current (post-2025-04-08) asymmetric regime cannot separate them, and would report
H_band while measuring H_side.

**The separator exists** — IDX's **2023-09-04 → 2025-04-07 symmetric regime** (LC-PM-0009). Under
symmetric bands H_band predicts the asymmetry **switches off**; H_side predicts it **persists**.
That is the discriminating test, and it is available only because the rule changed twice.

## transportability_condition

Requires (a) a dated, exogenous limit change with an untreated control — **IDX has two dated changes
but no cross-sectional control**, since the rule applies market-wide to all three boards
simultaneously. IDX must therefore use a **time-series / regime DiD**, which is weaker than
ChiNext's cross-sectional DiD and more exposed to concurrent macro shocks; and (b) a market where
manipulation risk and short-selling constraints resemble ChiNext's — IDX plausibly matches or
exceeds both.

## weakest_link

**No cross-sectional control group exists on IDX.** The band change applies to every ordinary
regular-market stock at once. The only candidate controls are imperfect: Papan Pemantauan Khusus
(different mechanism entirely — LC-PM-0006 Fact 3, and therefore *not* a valid control), or
price-tier variation in ARA width (Rp50–200 at 35% vs >Rp5,000 at 20%) **while ARB is uniform at
15%** — which does give within-regime cross-sectional variation in *asymmetry magnitude*. That
tier-based variation is the most promising control the sweep identified, and it is untested.

## biases[]

- **B6 · Period selection** — the 2020-08 window overlaps the post-COVID Chinese retail surge.
- **B2 · Selection** — authors state **"lower limit hits exhibit relatively insufficient sample
  data"** historically. If lower-limit hits are rare in China at ±10%, an IDX ARB study at −15% must
  demonstrate its own event count rather than assume it.
- **B8** — moderate.
- Generalisability explicitly caveated by the authors as emerging-market-specific.

## barrier_statement

**M6 at origin.** The rule is published and unarbitrageable. But the paper's contribution is about
**market quality**, not about a harvestable premium — it reports no tradeable return series and no
cost treatment. Under **EX6/R7.1** that is appropriate (profit is not evidence), but it also means
this card supplies a *mechanism and a design*, **not** a reason to expect an exploitable edge.

## limitations

Mechanism thinly specified; lower-limit sample sparse; no transaction-cost treatment; DiD control
unavailable in the IDX setting.
