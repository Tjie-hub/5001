# Gap 1 Closed: Sector Labels — and the dividend finding does not survive them

**Date:** 2026-09-19 · **Scripts:** `scripts/{fetch_sector,sector_test}.py` ·
**Data:** `data/sector_map.pkl`

## 1. What was acquired

780 tickers, **0 failures**, **98.6% labelled** — 11 sectors, 109 industries.
This replaces the previous state in which `candidate_watchlist_snapshot.sector`
was 100% NULL and `engine/sector_rotation.py` covered 82 of 959 names (8.5%),
which made sector concentration **the largest untestable confound in the program**.

Sector composition of the clean panel: Industrials 15.9%, Consumer Cyclical 11.9%,
Consumer Defensive 14.4%, Financial Services 12.9%, Basic Materials 11.7%,
Real Estate 7.9%, Energy 8.4%, Communication Services 6.4%, Healthcare 4.9%,
Technology 3.4%, Utilities 2.2%.

## 2. The headline: dividend yield was mostly a sector bet

| construction | pooled | **sector-neutral** |
|---|---|---|
| volatility tail exclusion | +0.275%/mo (t 4.24) | **+0.194%/mo (t 3.22)** |
| **dividend yield top-20%** | +0.909%/mo (t 2.29) | **+0.353%/mo (t 1.03)** |

**Dividend yield collapses under sector neutrality.** Its excess falls by 61%, its
t from 2.29 to 1.03, and its hit rate from 60% to 48% — i.e. to a coin flip. The
effect was substantially *which sectors pay dividends*, not *which companies within
a sector pay more*.

Sector-neutral the dividend book is **+4.24%/yr**, below the 6.25% deposit hurdle.

**This supersedes the standing conclusion.** The candidate reported as "the best
the program has produced" (+10.75%/yr hedged, Sharpe 0.94) rests on a factor that
is ~61% sector composition. The hedged construction was never tested
sector-neutral because the labels did not exist; it must be before any further use.

## 3. The volatility overlay survives

+0.275 → +0.194%/mo, **t 4.24 → 3.22**. It loses ~30% of its magnitude to sector
composition and retains the rest as a genuine within-sector effect. This is
consistent with, and strictly stronger than, the earlier indirect test — which
showed the excluded decile was *less* co-moving than a random basket (t −12.41)
but could not attribute composition.

Excluded-decile sector tilt is mild: Industrials +5.6pp, Technology +3.7pp,
Consumer Cyclical +3.6pp, against Financial Services −5.6pp and Energy −3.1pp.
Sector HHI 0.123 for the excluded set versus 0.112 for the universe — a tilt, not
a bet.

## 4. No sector factor of its own

Top-sector minus bottom-sector by trailing 12-month return: +0.92%/mo but
**t 0.92** over 49 months. No tradeable sector-momentum effect.

## 5. Consequence

The one previously-untestable confound turned out to matter, and it matters
against the program's best candidate rather than for it. Two actions follow:

1. **Re-run the hedged dividend book sector-neutral** before it is treated as a
   candidate at all. The draft protocol `FWD-PM-DIVYIELD-001` must not be
   registered until this is done — its signal may be ~60% sector tilt.
2. **Volatility exclusion should be constructed sector-neutral going forward.**
   It costs ~30% of the raw increment and removes a confound worth more than that.
