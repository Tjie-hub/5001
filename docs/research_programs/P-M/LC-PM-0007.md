**This is the strongest mechanism card in the M6/I1 sweep.** It is the only source found that
specifies a *complete* causal chain from the limit rule to a next-session price response, names the
participant classes with account-level data, and validates it against a rule-change experiment.

## identified_mechanisms

**Sub-class:** M6 (market design) originating the constraint, exploited through an **M5 attention /
coordination** channel. Maps to **I1 · price-limit pinning**, confounded with **I10 · limited
attention** exactly as [[MARKET_INEFFICIENCY_TAXONOMY]] warns.

**Causal chain (constraint → observable):**
news pushes price toward the upper limit → **large investors recognise they can push the close to
exactly the limit** → the limit-hit becomes a *salient public event* → uninformed investors read
limit-hitting as an undervaluation signal and buy the **next session** → large investors sell into
that demand → price rises 1–2 days, then **reverses over weeks to months**.

**Participant classes (account-level, not inferred):**
- **Large investors** (balance > ¥10m): buy day 0, sell day 1. **83.6%** executed this pattern.
  Acquire **0.69%** of floating shares on day 0, sell **0.51%** on day 1, against an average daily
  turnover of 0.44% for the group.
- **Small/medium investors** (< ¥5m): the mirror — sell day 0, buy day 1. Disposition effect plus
  attention-driven arrival.

**Authors' own scope note:** *"the speculators do not need to plan a manipulation scheme as they can
simply follow large price increases induced by fundamental news."* The mechanism does not require
intent.

## empirical_claims *(horizon calibration only — §4.1)*

After an **upper**-limit hit:

| Window | Return | t |
|---|---|---|
| **Close → next open** | **+2.44%** | 20.3 |
| Next open → next close | −0.30% | −4.0 |
| Day 2 | +0.45% | 4.1 |
| Days 6–10 | −0.59% | −6.4 |
| Days 21–60 | **−1.31%** | −4.7 |
| Days 61–120 | −0.97% | −7.1 |

**Control:** days with 9–10% returns that did *not* hit the limit show **−0.51%** overnight — no
continuation. **The discontinuity is at the limit, not at the return magnitude.** This is the single
most important number in the card: it separates the *rule* from the *move*.

**market/context:** Shenzhen Stock Exchange, 2012–2015 account-level (1997–2015 robustness);
1,628 A-shares; **21,005 upper-limit hits**, 2,046 ST-stock hits.
**rule design:** symmetric ±10% regular, ±5% ST.
**horizon:** overnight to day 2 (continuation); weeks to months (reversal).

## Mechanical vs information — how they separate it

**ST-assignment quasi-natural experiment.** A stock's limit tightens 10% → 5% on official ST
(distress) assignment, but *"the market can well anticipate the ST assignment"* 2–3 months earlier.
Same firm, same information state, **different limit regime**. Result: the buy-day-0/sell-day-1
pattern by large investors appears **only after** ST assignment.

**This is the design template for IDX** — a dated, exogenous, anticipated-in-information but
discontinuous-in-rule change. IDX has two such switches (LC-PM-0009).

## transportability_condition

Three conditions. **Two hold on IDX; one is the crux.**

1. ✅ **Retail-dominated, attention-driven investor base** — holds.
2. ✅ **Short-selling effectively unavailable**, so the mechanism runs on the **upper** limit only.
   The authors state SZSE short interest was *"less than 0.005% of tradable shares"*. IDX's
   short-selling is a restricted eligibility list, not a retail instrument (LC-PM-0006). **The
   asymmetry in Xiong is caused by the shorting constraint, NOT by band-width asymmetry.**
3. ❓ **Fill feasibility at the limit close.** The profit accrues to whoever is filled *at* the
   limit. Account data show large investors *are* filled — because they are the ones pushing it.

## weakest_link

**The +2.44% is close-to-open and therefore not capturable by a participant who was not already
filled at the limit close.** At ARA the buy queue is enormous and a late arrival does not execute —
the mechanism that creates the effect is the same one that excludes you from it. The subsequent
reversal (−1.31% days 21–60) *is* real but requires **shorting**, which IDX does not practically
permit. **On the upper-limit side this mechanism is real, large, and un-harvestable in both
directions** — structurally the same trap as HYP-PA-0001's ADD side.

> **The corollary is the finding that matters:** on the **lower** limit the execution asymmetry
> **inverts**. At ARB the queue is composed of *sellers*; a buyer supplying liquidity into that wall
> can be filled, and the subsequent reversal is **upward and long-only**. This card does not
> establish that effect — SZSE's lower-limit dynamics are barely studied here (LC-PM-0008 notes
> lower-limit hits are historically data-poor) — but it identifies where an executable version could
> exist.

## biases[]

- **B4 · Retro-fitted mechanism** — **not present**; the ST experiment is designed ex ante.
- **B5 · Multiple testing** — authors report R² of **0.000–0.004** in predictive regressions; large
  unexplained variance.
- **B2 · Selection into limit-hit sample** — acknowledged; mitigated within-firm.
- **B8 · Market-structure obsolescence** — moderate; SZSE 2012–2015.
- **Sample size** — 119 ST stocks over four years is thin for the causal leg.

## barrier_statement

**M6 structural at the origin; M5 behavioural at the exploitation.** The rule cannot be arbitraged
away. But the *profit* is compensation for coordination capacity and inventory risk borne by large
investors — under §6.3 that is closer to fair compensation than to a durable inefficiency available
to an outside participant. Authors are explicit they **cannot separate** investor naivety, attention,
and coordination channels. **F1 exposure is real on the exploitation half even though the origin
half is M6.**

## limitations

Channels not separately identified; low R²; ST sample thin; no bid-ask spread or depth reported;
SZSE-specific institutional detail.
