## identified_mechanisms

**Sub-class:** M1 inventory, extended to **cross-stock** propagation. **I5**, with an I6 (illiquidity)
interaction.

**Causal chain:** individuals generate a **non-informational** imbalance in stock *i* → a *finite
number* of risk-averse liquidity providers (CARA, risk-aversion λ) absorb it → they become short *i*
and **hedge by buying correlated stocks j, k** → prices of *j, k* rise contemporaneously **even with
no trading in j** → the resulting inventory must later be unwound → **predictable reversal in i and
in the hedge names.**

**Participant class:** competitive risk-averse liquidity providers. **Constraint:** finite capital
and finite risk-bearing capacity — *not* a designated obligation. **This is the version of the
mechanism that can transport to a market with no specialist.**

**Non-informational identification — four independent legs:**
- 99.3% of margin-account trades are by individuals;
- the margin portfolio **underperforms** (−10.97 bp/week; α = −9.74 bp/week) → not informed;
- 80% of margin trades are aggressive/liquidity-demanding;
- imbalance autocorrelation is not rejected as zero for 76% of stocks.

## empirical_claims *(horizon calibration only)*

| Horizon | Reversal magnitude |
|---|---|
| 1 week | **52 bp** |
| 4 weeks | 92 bp |
| 10 weeks | 125 bp |

Lagged (t−1) imbalance coefficient: **−0.3297 (t = −7.76)**.

**market/context:** Taiwan Stock Exchange, Jan 1994 – Aug 2002, 451 weeks, 607 companies.
Order-driven; retail turnover 3–6× portfolio/yr; 8.42% of shares outstanding in margin accounts.
**horizon:** **weekly and longer** — the paper does not establish a 1–3 day effect.

## transportability_condition

**Strong on structure, weak on instrument.** Taiwan matches IDX on the load-bearing features:
order-driven, no specialist, retail-dominated, emerging-adjacent. But the mechanism is identified
**through a margin-account instrument that IDX does not publish**. TSE disseminates aggregated
margin holdings in real time across all brokers; without an equivalent, IDX cannot separate
non-informational from informed flow, and the paper's own separation collapses into LIM2.

The authors themselves flag this: *"organizational differences may limit direct replication in
markets without consolidated margin reporting."*

## weakest_link

**52 bp per week is below IDX's 60 bp round-trip friction.** Even taking the effect at face value and
assuming full transport, the flagship weekly result **fails F4 on arrival**. Only the 4-week (92 bp)
and 10-week (125 bp) horizons clear friction — and those are far outside the 1–3 day window this
Edge is exploring, and outside PM-0002's k≤15 as well.

## biases[]

- **B3 · Backfill** — controlled: returns measured from the **opening price of the week following**
  the sort, explicitly to exclude bid-ask bounce. Good practice; worth inheriting.
- **B6 · Period selection** — 1994–2002; TSE microstructure has changed since.
- **B8 · Market-structure obsolescence** — moderate.
- **B5 · Multiple testing within paper** — conditional double-sorts on return level and turnover;
  reported robustness is a grid.

## barrier_statement

**Limits to arbitrage — the strongest barrier of the M2.x set.** Persistence is explained by the
*finite risk-bearing capacity and finite capital* of liquidity providers, not by an information
asymmetry. Under §6.3 this is a genuine D3 barrier: capital does not arrive instantly. The authors
leave provider identity, capital constraints, and entry barriers *"for future study"* — so the
barrier is **asserted, not measured.**

## limitations

Provider identity and capital constraints unstudied; margin-trading halts create excluded
sub-periods; instrument unavailable outside Taiwan; weekly granularity only.
