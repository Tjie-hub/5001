> **Q1, and recorded anyway.** It is the **only Indonesia-specific study of an IDX auto-rejection
> regime change** located in this sweep. Under §3.1 a Q1 card *"names a mechanism but does not
> specify it"* and may **not** source a registration. Its value here is (a) establishing that the
> Indonesia-specific literature on this event is essentially empty, and (b) supplying a weak prior.

## identified_mechanisms

**None specified.** The study is a market-*reaction* event study around a rule change; it proposes no
causal chain, names no constrained participant class, and states no falsifiable mechanism. On the
§2.2 criteria this is close to **EX1** (an effect with no mechanism). It is retained as an
Indonesia-context data point, not as mechanism evidence.

## empirical_claims

**event:** the R2 → R3 transition — asymmetric ARB 15% → **symmetric** auto-rejection (2023-09-04
per LC-PM-0009; the paper itself references 2023-06-05 for the 15% step and does not state the
symmetric date precisely).
**method:** event study — Shapiro–Wilk normality, then **Wilcoxon signed-rank** (data non-normal),
plus one-sample t-tests, on abnormal return and trading volume.
**sample:** stated population 55 companies; **5 companies** selected by simple random sampling.
Observation window not specified.

**findings:**
- **No** significant difference in abnormal return before vs after the change.
- **Significant** difference in **trading volume**.

## transportability_condition

Trivially satisfied — it *is* IDX. The problem is not transportability but power and specification.

## weakest_link

**n = 5.** A five-stock sample cannot detect anything but an enormous effect, so the null on abnormal
return is **uninformative** — it is not evidence of absence. The volume result is directionally
consistent with the price-limit literature (limits bind → volume redistributes) but at this sample
size is close to anecdote.

Compounding: the observation window is unstated, so it cannot be verified whether the event window
straddles the R2/R3 boundary cleanly (LC-PM-0009 requires it to).

## biases[]

- **B5 · Multiple testing within paper** — three tests (Shapiro–Wilk, Wilcoxon, t-test) on two
  variables, no correction stated.
- **B6 · Period selection** — window unspecified, therefore unassessable.
- **B2 · Selection** — 5 of 55 by simple random sampling; no liquidity, board or price-tier control,
  so Papan Pemantauan Khusus and IPO contamination cannot be excluded (LC-PM-0006 Fact 3).
- **B7 · Cost omission** — no cost treatment.
- **B1 · Publication bias** — a regional journal reporting a null on the headline variable is
  *mildly* reassuring on B1, being the kind of result the published record usually suppresses.

## barrier_statement

**None offered.** The paper makes no persistence claim. Under **Rule I-1**, if no card supplies a
barrier, synthesis fails — so this card **cannot** contribute to the barrier statement for any
successor hypothesis. LC-PM-0009 must carry it.

## limitations

Q1 grade; n=5; window unstated; no mechanism; no cost treatment; not indexed. **Usable only as a
search lead and as evidence that the Indonesia-specific literature on this rule change is
effectively absent** — which is itself relevant under §6.4, since capital-scarce and under-studied
is exactly where the standard says to look.
